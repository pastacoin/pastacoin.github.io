Does the median transaction track purchasing power? Five chains

The Bitcoin page found that the average on-chain transaction got 150 times larger in real terms, which breaks the whitepaper's gauge. Averages are dominated by exchanges and whales. This page repeats the test on every chain with free daily data, using the median transaction wherever it is available.

6. Caveats

  • Two sources. Litecoin, Dogecoin and Bitcoin Cash use Blockchair's daily aggregates, where transfer value is the total output of each transaction including the change a wallet sends back to itself; levels overstate true transfers, the median far less than the mean. Bitcoin and Ethereum use BitInfoCharts' daily median and mean transaction value in USD, converted to coin units at the CoinMetrics daily price, because Blockchair rate-limits bulk pulls. The two sources agree on Litecoin's yearly medians to within about 20%.
  • Ethereum: the median is only available until 2019, after which zero-value contract calls swamp it. The mean is diluted by those calls. ERC-20 and stablecoin transfers, which carry most of Ethereum's payment activity today, are not counted anywhere here. A filtered median (plain transfers of at least 0.001 ETH) is queued behind Blockchair's limits.
  • Dust and inscriptions: periods of spam with near-zero outputs (Dogecoin 2024, Bitcoin Cash 2019) drag the daily median toward zero. Those months show as dips in the USD chart and weaken the fits.
  • Round-number habits: Dogecoin's median sat at exactly 10,000 DOGE for years because of tipping conventions. A convention, not a price signal.
  • Active addresses count addresses, not people; exchanges and batching make it a loose proxy for users.
